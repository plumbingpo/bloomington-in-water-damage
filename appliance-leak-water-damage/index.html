Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Bloomington

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

For homeowners in Bloomington, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. In laundry rooms it means the wall behind the machine and the rooms next door, or the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs. Then our team removes the standing water, meters the materials that soaked it up, and places equipment matched to the size of the damage.

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut the Machine DownIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. A refrigerator line has a small valve below or behind the unit.
  3. Mop Up What You CanGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Leave the Door ShutA dishwasher or washer that still holds water should stay closed. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Document What FailedTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.

How We Handle Appliance Leak Water Damage

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Water under a refrigerator from a blocked defrost drain or a drain pan with a crack in it.
  • An overflow caused by an overloaded drum or by using too much detergent.
  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
  • Plastic or copper ice maker lines that kink, crack or loosen at the fitting.
  • A failed water inlet valve that keeps filling the washer or dishwasher after it should stop.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Flooring and Subfloor

Water slides beneath laminate, vinyl plank and hardwood. The boards swell or cup, while the subfloor below remains wet well after the top looks dry.

Cabinet Damage

Dishwasher leaks soak cabinet sides and toe kicks from underneath. The panels swell and separate, and wet cabinet boxes can hold moisture against the wall for weeks.

Mold Behind Appliances

Behind a refrigerator or a dishwasher it is warm and dark, and nobody checks there often. Long before it is discovered, a slow drip back there can be growing mold across the floor and wall.

Adjoining Rooms

For hours, a failed washer hose can keep releasing water. Hallways, bedrooms and closets get wet as it travels under the walls, and carpet and pad are soaked a good distance from the laundry.

Insurance

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. How old the hose was and how it was maintained may also come up with some insurers. Before cleanup, take photos, save the failed part and read your policy.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • Any mold growth, because it must be dealt with ahead of drying.
  • If the flooring and cabinets have to come out or can be dried where they are.
  • If it was gray discharge, which must be sanitized, or clean water from the supply.
  • Which appliance leaked and whether it was a fast flood or a slow seep.
