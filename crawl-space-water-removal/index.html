Crawl Space Water Removal: What Bloomington Homeowners Should Know

Water under the house is easy to miss. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. Usually that means days of moisture in the joists already, plus insulation that is wet all the way through. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Pumping is just half of the work. Because lumber under a floor is slow to soak and slow to dry, our crew tracks its moisture content with a meter and runs dehumidification for the 3 to 5 days it normally takes to bring the readings down. Our team traces the source too, whether that is a supply line leak, a drain, or rain driven groundwater, so the space stays dry afterward.

Signs to Look For

  • Wood flooring on the first level that has begun to curl up along the seams
  • Fiberglass insulation sagging or hanging down between the floor joists
  • Steel posts, hangers for pipes and duct straps showing orange rust
  • A first floor with a cool, moist feel and some give when you walk on it
  • An odor like damp soil that gets stronger close to the registers
  • Ducts and plumbing lines below the house covered in condensation
If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First day

Puddles Form Underneath

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

Hours 24 to 48

Early Mold Growth

Mold can start on damp joists and paper faced insulation. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

After a week

Wood Starts Rotting

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Weakened Floor Framing

The first soft wood is usually hidden, up where joists meet the subfloor or back at the sill plate. We probe the wood to find soft spots.

Settling Under the House

Long periods of standing water loosen the soil that holds up the support piers. Doors that stick and floors that slope upstairs are possible signs.

What You Breathe Upstairs

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

Water That Is Not Clean

What collects beneath a home is frequently dirty. If it came from a drain pipe or from waste laden groundwater it is rated Category 2 or 3, so we suit up and disinfect.

Our Process

How We Handle Crawl Space Water Removal

1

Inspect and Make Safe

Before a technician goes under the house, we look for live electrical dangers, fallen ductwork and dirty water. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Pump Out Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. Mud and silt left on the liner are cleared at the same time.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Dry and Verify

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.
